What does ORM stand for, and what kind of modeling method is it?	Object-Role Modeling.<br>A <b>fact-based</b> (fact-oriented) conceptual modeling method: it describes a domain as objects playing roles in relationships (facts), independent of any implementation.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
What is the single most distinctive feature of ORM compared with ER and UML class diagrams?	It is <b>attribute-free</b>. Everything is modeled as an object playing a role in a relationship (a fact type). There are no attributes on entities.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
Give two concrete benefits of an attribute-free (fact-based) model.	1. Every fact is a full sentence, so a domain expert can validate it in natural language.<br>2. It is stable under change: adding information means adding a fact type, not restructuring an entity. What starts as an "attribute" can gain its own constraints and facts without a rewrite.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
What is an <i>elementary fact</i>?	A proposition that cannot be split into smaller facts without losing information. It asserts that one or more objects play roles in a single relationship, e.g. "Person 'Ada' was born in Country 'UK'".<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
Why does ORM insist on elementary facts as the atomic unit?	Because elementary facts are the smallest units a domain expert can affirm or deny as true, and constraints attach cleanly to them. Compound facts hide multiple assertions and multiple constraints, which obscures the model.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
What is the difference between a fact type and a fact instance?	A <b>fact type</b> is the relationship pattern (e.g. "Customer places Order"). A <b>fact instance</b> is one true occurrence of it in the population (e.g. "Customer 'Ada' places Order '1007'").<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
What is a <i>population</i> in ORM, and what is it for?	A set of sample fact instances attached to the model. It is used to validate the schema with domain experts and to check that the declared constraints actually hold on realistic data -- "do the examples actually work?"<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
What does "semantics first" mean as a design goal of Barwise?	The core representation is the ORM conceptual model -- the meaning of the domain. Physical decisions (star schema, Data Vault, normalized tables) are downstream concerns derived from it, never baked into it.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
What is verbalization, and which tradition does Barwise's phrasing follow?	Verbalization is the automatic rendering of fact types and constraints as natural-language sentences. Barwise follows the <b>FORML</b> (Formal ORM Language) patterns established by Halpin.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
Name the seven steps of the Conceptual Schema Design Procedure (CSDP), at least the first three.	1. Transform familiar examples into elementary facts.<br>2. Draw the fact types and populate with sample data.<br>3. Check for combinable/splittable fact types.<br>4. Add uniqueness constraints and check arities.<br>5. Add mandatory-role constraints; check for logical derivations.<br>6. Add value, set-comparison, and other constraints.<br>7. Check for consistency and redundancy against the examples.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
In the CSDP, what is the very first thing you do, and with whose help?	Transform <i>familiar examples</i> -- concrete sample data, reports, or forms from the domain -- into elementary facts, working with a domain expert who can confirm each fact is true.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
Why is "verbalize back to the domain expert" a core validation loop in fact-based modeling?	Because the expert cannot read a diagram or schema, but can judge whether a plain sentence like "Each Order is placed by at most one Customer" is true. Verbalization turns the model into something the expert can sign off on.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
In Barwise, what makes non-determinism (LLM calls, clocks, network) stay out of the core?	A design principle: validation, verbalization, mapping, diff, and query are pure and deterministic -- same input, same output. Non-deterministic work lives in the outer packages (llm, cli, mcp, vscode).<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- conceptual modeling first steps and the CSDP.</i>
