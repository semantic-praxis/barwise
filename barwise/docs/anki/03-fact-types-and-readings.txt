What is a <i>fact type</i> in ORM?	A relationship between object types, expressed as an ordered set of roles. "Customer places Order" is a binary fact type with two roles.
What is a <i>role</i>?	A position within a fact type, played by an object type. Each role carries a role name used in verbalization. In "Customer places Order", "places" and "is placed by" are the role phrasings.
Define the <i>arity</i> of a fact type and name the arities ORM supports.	Arity is the number of roles. ORM supports <b>unary</b> (1), <b>binary</b> (2, most common), <b>ternary</b> (3), and higher-arity fact types.
Give an example of a <i>unary</i> fact type and say what it captures.	"Person smokes" or "Account is active." A unary fact type records whether an object plays a single role at all -- a boolean-like property, but modeled as a fact, not an attribute.
When is a ternary fact type genuinely needed rather than two binaries?	When the three roles are mutually irreducible -- splitting into binaries would lose information. Example: "Person scored Mark in Subject." You cannot split it without losing which mark goes with which subject.
What is a <i>reading</i> (reading order) of a fact type?	An ordered sequence of roles with interstitial text that produces a natural-language reading. "Customer places Order" is the forward reading; "Order is placed by Customer" is the inverse.
How many readings must a fact type have, and how many does a binary usually have?	At least one (the forward reading) -- a structural validation rule. A binary usually has two: forward and inverse, so it verbalizes naturally from either object's point of view.
A structural rule says "every fact type has at least one reading order." Why does that matter for the whole toolkit?	Because verbalization, diagramming, and constraint sentences all build on the reading. A fact type with no reading cannot be turned into a sentence a domain expert can check.
In a reading template like <code>{0} places {1}</code>, what do <code>{0}</code> and <code>{1}</code> refer to?	The role players by position: {0} is the object type in role 0, {1} the object type in role 1. Verbalizers expand the placeholders with the players' names.
What is the relationship between roles and constraints?	Constraints attach to roles (or sequences of roles). A uniqueness constraint covers one or more roles of a fact type; a mandatory constraint covers a single role. Roles are the anchor points for every business rule.
A completeness warning fires for a fact type with no constraints. What does that usually mean?	That the modeler has not finished specifying the business rules. Almost every real relationship has at least a uniqueness constraint fixing its multiplicity; none is a sign of unfinished work.
