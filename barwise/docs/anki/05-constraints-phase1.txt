Name the four Phase 1 (core) constraint types in Barwise.	Internal uniqueness, mandatory role, external uniqueness, value constraint.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
What does an <b>internal uniqueness</b> constraint assert, and what does it apply to?	Applies to one or more roles <i>within a single fact type</i>. Asserts that the combination of values in those roles is unique across the population -- no two fact instances repeat that combination.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
On a binary fact type, what does a single-role uniqueness constraint tell you about multiplicity?	That the relationship is <b>many-to-one</b> (functional) on that side. "Each Order is placed by at most one Customer" means the Order role is unique -- one customer per order, many orders per customer.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
What does a <b>mandatory role</b> constraint assert, and what does it apply to?	Applies to a single role. Asserts that every instance of the object type playing that role must participate -- "Each Customer places at least one Order." No customer may exist without placing an order.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
Uniqueness constrains "at most one"; mandatory constrains "at least one". What does declaring both on the same role give you?	Exactly one: the role is both mandatory (at least one) and unique (at most one), i.e. a total function. Every instance participates exactly once.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
How does <b>external uniqueness</b> differ from internal uniqueness?	External uniqueness spans roles from <i>different</i> fact types; internal uniqueness covers roles within one fact type. External uniqueness is how a compound reference scheme (identify a Room by Building + RoomNumber) is expressed.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
What does a <b>value constraint</b> restrict, and what two forms can it take?	Restricts the allowed values of a value type or role. Two forms: an <b>enumeration</b> ({'A','B','C'}) or a <b>range</b> (between 1 and 10; at least 18).<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
Which validation rule set checks that a uniqueness constraint's roles actually exist in the fact type it names?	Constraint consistency. It also checks that subset/equality constraints reference role sequences of compatible arity and type.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
What is population validation, in one line?	It checks that the declared sample populations actually satisfy every declared constraint -- the runtime "do the examples work?" check.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
You see the sentence "Each Product has at most one Price." Which constraint produced it, on which role?	An internal uniqueness constraint, single-role, on the role played by <b>Product</b> in the binary "Product has Price".<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
You see "The possible values of Priority are: {'low', 'medium', 'high'}." Which constraint is this?	A value constraint (enumeration form) on the value type or role "Priority".<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 4 (uniqueness; external uniqueness is 4.4), section 5.2 (mandatory roles), 6.3 (value constraints); ORM2-02.</i>
