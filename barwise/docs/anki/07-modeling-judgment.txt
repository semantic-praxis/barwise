[Decompose] Is "Person scored Mark in Subject" reducible to "Person scored Mark" + "Person took Subject"? Answer, then grade against the back.	No -- it is already elementary. <br><br><b>Core point:</b> splitting loses the pairing of which mark goes with which subject. A ternary is irreducible when no combination of binaries preserves the same information. <b>Full credit:</b> you rejected the split <i>and</i> named the lost association.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- the CSDP (elementary facts; checking for redundancy).</i>
[Spot the smell] A model declares entity type "Employee" and a note says it has "salary: number". What is wrong, and how do you fix it in ORM?	<b>Smell:</b> an attribute. ORM is attribute-free. <br><b>Fix:</b> model a fact type "Employee earns Salary" with a value type Salary (e.g. a MonetaryAmount). <br><br><b>Core point:</b> everything is a fact. <b>Full credit:</b> you also noted the payoff -- Salary can now carry its own facts and constraints (currency, effective date) with no restructuring.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- the CSDP (elementary facts; checking for redundancy).</i>
[Spot the smell] The fact type "Person has EmailAddress" has no constraints. Which business rule is almost certainly missing?	At least a <b>uniqueness</b> constraint fixing multiplicity: is it one email per person, one person per email, or both? Often also a mandatory role. <br><br><b>Core point:</b> a fact type with no constraint is unfinished (a completeness warning fires). <b>Full credit:</b> you asked which side is unique rather than assuming.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- the CSDP (elementary facts; checking for redundancy).</i>
[Judgment call] Should "Color" be an entity type or a value type? What decides it?	It depends on whether Color <i>plays roles beyond being named</i>. <br>- Just a label on things -> <b>value type</b>.<br>- Has its own facts (hex code, is-in Palette, has-name) -> <b>entity type</b> with a reference scheme.<br><br><b>Core point:</b> the test is "does it need its own facts/identity?", not "does it feel like a thing?". <b>Full credit:</b> you gave the test, not just a pick.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- the CSDP (elementary facts; checking for redundancy).</i>
[Judgment call] "Student enrolled in Course" now needs a grade and an enrollment date. What ORM move do you make, and why?	<b>Objectify</b> the fact type into "Enrollment", then attach "Enrollment has Grade" and "Enrollment has Date". <br><br><b>Core point:</b> when you need to say something <i>about a fact</i>, turn the fact into an object. <b>Full credit:</b> you chose objectification over inventing an "EnrollmentRecord" entity with duplicated identifying facts.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- the CSDP (elementary facts; checking for redundancy).</i>
[Judgment call] Sales calls any lead a "Customer"; billing calls only paying parties "Customer". How does Barwise let both coexist without collision?	Model them in <b>separate bounded contexts</b> (e.g. crm and billing), each owning its own Customer. Relate them with a <b>context mapping</b> using the <code>anticorruption_layer</code> pattern and record a <code>semantic_conflict</code> documenting the two meanings and the resolution. <br><br><b>Core point:</b> same word, different concept -> separate namespaces + an explicit mapping, never one shared "Customer". <b>Full credit:</b> you named the anticorruption-layer / semantic-conflict mechanism, not just "use two models".<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- the CSDP (elementary facts; checking for redundancy).</i>
