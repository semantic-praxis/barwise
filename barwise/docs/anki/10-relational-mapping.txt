What does the relational mapper (Rmap) do, and what is its input and output?	It transforms the conceptual ORM model (or OIAL output) into a logical relational schema -- tables, columns, keys, and foreign keys -- which can then be rendered as DDL, dbt model skeletons, or other physical targets.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
In ORM's philosophy, what is the relationship between the conceptual model and the relational schema?	The conceptual model is the source of truth; the relational schema is <i>derived</i> from it. Physical decisions live downstream and never leak back into the meaning. Change the facts, and the schema is re-derived.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
A binary fact type with single-role uniqueness maps to what relational structure?	A foreign key on the table representing the <b>uniqueness side</b>. "Each Order is placed by at most one Customer" -> Order table gets a customer_id foreign key.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
A binary fact type with spanning uniqueness (both roles unique) maps to what, and what decides?	Either a foreign key or a separate table -- the choice depends on the mandatory constraints. A one-to-one that is mandatory on one side can absorb; otherwise a separate table may be cleaner.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
Ternary and higher-arity fact types map to what?	An associative table, with columns for each role player and a key over the uniqueness-constrained roles.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
How does a mandatory-role constraint show up in the generated schema?	As a NOT NULL on the corresponding column. Mandatory ("at least one") means the value must be present; the column cannot be null.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
How does a value constraint typically show up in the generated schema?	As a CHECK constraint (for a range or enumeration) or an equivalent enum type -- the allowed values from the conceptual model become a physical guard.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
Subtyping maps to one of which three strategies, and what drives the choice?	Absorption into the supertype table, separate tables per subtype, or partition. The Rmap picks based on the constraint pattern (e.g. exclusive/exhaustive subtypes favor a partition).<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
What is the OIAL layer, and why does it sit between the model and the schema?	The Object-oriented Information Analysis Layer: an intermediate abstraction that resolves ORM-specific constructs (objectification, subtyping, multi-role uniqueness) into a simplified object graph, so the same conceptual model can target multiple physical schemas.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
[Judgment] Your ORM model has "Person has at most one PrimaryEmail" and "Person has EmailAddress" (many). A teammate wants one nullable email column. What does the mapping say, and what is lost if you flatten?	Rmap gives PrimaryEmail a foreign key / column on Person (single-role uniqueness) and EmailAddress a separate table (no uniqueness on the Person side -> one-to-many). <br><br><b>Core point:</b> flattening to one column collapses a one-to-many into a one-to-one and silently drops the "many emails" fact. <b>Full credit:</b> you traced each fact type's multiplicity to its table shape before agreeing.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- ch. 11 (Relational Mapping; the Rmap procedure is section 11.3); barwise ARCHITECTURE.md sections 3.4-3.5.</i>
