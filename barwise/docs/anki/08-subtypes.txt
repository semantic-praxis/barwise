What is a <b>SubtypeFact</b> in ORM?	A specialization relationship: entity type A is a subtype of entity type B (e.g. Manager is a subtype of Employee). The subtype inherits the supertype's identification and can play roles the supertype cannot.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
[Produce the FORML sentence] Manager is declared a subtype of Employee. How does Barwise verbalize the subtype fact?	Manager is a subtype of Employee.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
[Produce the FORML sentence] Manager is a subtype of Employee, defined by the rule "Employee manages some Employee". How does Barwise verbalize it?	Manager is a subtype of Employee, defined as: Employee manages some Employee.<br><br>(When a SubtypeFact has a defining rule, its expression is appended after "defined as:".)<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
What is the difference between an <i>asserted</i> and a <i>derived (defined)</i> subtype?	An asserted subtype is populated by explicit assertion (someone says "this Employee is a Manager"). A derived subtype carries a <b>defining rule</b>: membership is computed from the rule (e.g. "Manager = Employee who manages some Employee").<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
Why model "Manager" as a subtype instead of adding an "is a manager" flag to Employee?	Because a subtype can play <i>roles the supertype cannot</i>. A Manager can "approves Budget"; a plain Employee cannot. A boolean flag cannot carry those manager-only facts; a subtype can.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
What does it mean for two subtypes to be <b>exclusive</b> (disjoint)?	No instance may belong to both at once -- e.g. an Account is not both a CheckingAccount and a SavingsAccount. It is expressed with an exclusion constraint over the subtype roles.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
What does it mean for a set of subtypes to be <b>exhaustive</b> (total)?	Every instance of the supertype belongs to at least one subtype -- e.g. every Account is either checking or savings. It is expressed with a disjunctive-mandatory (total) constraint.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
Combine exclusive and exhaustive over a set of subtypes. What does that give you?	A <b>partition</b>: every supertype instance belongs to exactly one subtype -- the subtypes neither overlap nor leave a gap.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
Name the three standard strategies for mapping subtyping to tables.	Absorption into the supertype table, separate tables per subtype, or partition (one table per subtype with no supertype table). Barwise's Rmap chooses based on the constraint pattern.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
[Judgment] A model has Employee with a subtype Manager, but Manager plays no roles of its own and has no defining rule. Is the subtype earning its place?	Probably not. <br><br><b>Core point:</b> a subtype justifies itself by (a) playing subtype-specific roles, or (b) carrying a defining rule that others reference. With neither, a unary "Employee is a manager" fact or a value constraint is simpler. <b>Full credit:</b> you asked "what can a Manager do that an Employee cannot?" before keeping it.<br><br><i>Read more: Halpin & Morgan 3rd ed. -- sections 6.5-6.6 (subtyping and generalization), 10.8 (further constraints involving subtyping).</i>
