Facilitator: Let's go through how appointments work at the clinic.

Practice Manager: Patients book appointments. Every appointment is booked by exactly one patient, and a patient can have many appointments.

Facilitator: Who does the patient see?

Practice Manager: Each appointment is with exactly one doctor. Doctors see many patients across their appointments.

Facilitator: How are patients and doctors identified?

Practice Manager: Patients have a patient ID -- it's on their card. Doctors have a license number. Appointments get an appointment number when they're created. Exam rooms have a room number.

Facilitator: What do you record about a doctor?

Practice Manager: Every doctor has exactly one specialty, like cardiology or general practice.

Facilitator: And about the appointment itself?

Practice Manager: Every appointment is on a date and at a time slot -- we book in fixed slots like 09:00 or 09:30. Every appointment also has a status. The status is one of: scheduled, checked-in, completed, or cancelled.

Facilitator: Is the status ever missing?

Practice Manager: Never. An appointment always has exactly one status from that list.

Facilitator: Anything optional?

Practice Manager: An appointment can have a visit reason the patient gives when booking, and after the visit the doctor can attach a follow-up note. At most one follow-up note per appointment.

Facilitator: One more thing -- do patients have a regular doctor?

Practice Manager: Yes, every patient has exactly one primary doctor. That's separate from who they happen to see in a given appointment.
