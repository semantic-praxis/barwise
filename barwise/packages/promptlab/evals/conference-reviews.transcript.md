Facilitator: Walk me through how paper review works for the conference.

Program Chair: Reviewers review papers. A reviewer can review many papers, and each paper gets several reviewers, but a given reviewer reviews a given paper at most once.

Facilitator: How is everything identified?

Program Chair: Reviewers have a reviewer ID. Papers have a paper number. And each completed review gets its own review ID -- we treat the review itself as a thing we track.

Facilitator: What do you record about a review?

Program Chair: Every review has exactly one overall score. The score is always one of 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5.

Facilitator: Is the score ever missing?

Program Chair: No. A review without a score is not a review -- it always has exactly one.

Facilitator: So the review connects the reviewer and the paper?

Program Chair: Exactly. The review is the act of a particular reviewer reviewing a particular paper -- the score hangs off that pairing, not off the paper alone.
