Facilitator: Walk me through how enrollment works at the university.

Registrar: Students enroll in course offerings, not in courses directly. A course offering is a specific course offered in a specific semester.

Facilitator: So the same course can have multiple offerings?

Registrar: Right. Each offering is of exactly one course and belongs to exactly one semester. Intro to Databases might run in both Fall and Spring.

Facilitator: Who teaches an offering?

Registrar: Each offering is taught by exactly one instructor. An instructor can teach several offerings in the same semester.

Facilitator: How are these things identified?

Registrar: Students have a student number. Courses have a course code, like CS101. Offerings get an offering ID. Instructors have a staff ID, and semesters have a semester code like 2026-FA.

Facilitator: What do you track about a course itself?

Registrar: Every course has a title and a credit value. The credit value is a whole number, usually between 1 and 6.

Facilitator: Anything on the offering besides course and semester?

Registrar: Each offering has a maximum capacity -- a cap on how many students can enroll.

Facilitator: And semesters?

Registrar: A semester has a start date and an end date.

Facilitator: What about grades?

Registrar: At the end of a semester, a student receives a letter grade for each course offering they were enrolled in. The grade is one of A, B, C, D, or F. A student gets at most one grade per offering.
